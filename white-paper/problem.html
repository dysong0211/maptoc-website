White Paper / Understand MapToc

The problem MapToc solves

Why evidence reconstruction can separate a program result from the decisions and changes behind it.

Programs generate evidence continuously, but the institutional record often does not grow with the work. Updates remain scattered across folders, emails, reports and staff memory; some context is never recorded or becomes difficult to recover. By the time oversight, evaluation or audit preparation begins, reviewers may have to reconstruct how funding, decisions, delivery and outcomes relate.

The reconstruction problem

When reporting or evaluation begins, staff may need to locate source material, reconcile versions, ask colleagues what a change meant and repeat explanations that were already given elsewhere. If people have moved on, the reasoning behind a decision may be harder to recover than the decision itself.

This is the operational problem MapToc targets. The product does not assume that all programs are poorly planned or that missing public data means no work was done. It focuses on a narrower question: can teams preserve a more usable account of implementation without creating another substantial reporting obligation?

Four things can become separated

  • The intended change: what the program expected to achieve and for whom.
  • What happened: delivery observations, reported results and relevant feedback.
  • Why the approach changed: constraints, decisions, risks and adaptations.
  • What the evidence supports: the source, its coverage, uncertainty and competing explanations.

What a better starting point looks like

An evaluator should be able to inspect a dated, source-linked history and see where evidence is missing or disputed. Program teams should be able to reuse approved material while still interpreting and validating each report for its audience.

The ambition is a stronger starting point for judgement. It is not to remove investigation, guarantee complete coverage or automate an explanation that the evidence cannot support. Background studies and public-record research are presented separately in the Research collection and the homepage's The Need section.

The institutional accountability gap

Funders, governments and foundations need to know how resources were used, what work occurred and what changed. Yet a grant can close with financial statements and a final narrative while the events, approvals and evidence behind them have been scattered or lost as the program progressed. Staffing, travel and procurement totals may remain difficult to connect to their purpose, authorization, delivery and results.

MapToc addresses this loss of institutional memory by maintaining the explanation throughout implementation. Missing sources, unresolved variances and weak outcome evidence stay visible for review. They are questions to investigate, not automated findings of misuse.
