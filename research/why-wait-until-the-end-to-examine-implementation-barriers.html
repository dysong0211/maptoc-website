Examining implementation barriers before close-out

Implementation research can inform adjustments before closure. The reviewed CFIR applications do not establish the prevalence of late evaluation across all aid programs.

Evidence & Evaluation · Editorial briefSecondary synthesis · Not peer reviewed · Sources credited below

Key takeaway

Implementation research can inform adjustments before closure. The reviewed CFIR applications do not establish the prevalence of late evaluation across all aid programs.

Evidence and context

An explanation of implementation problems has particular value when someone can still act on it. The same observation may support a practical adjustment during delivery and a recommendation after the program ends.

A 2020 systematic review examined 34 studies using the Consolidated Framework for Implementation Research in 21 low- and middle-income countries. It found that 59% applied the framework at study endline, primarily for analysis or interpretation. That is a finding about the reviewed applications of one framework, not all program evaluation. Systematic review, Implementation Science, 2020

The finding raises a useful design question: what would a team need to recognise an implementation barrier earlier? A framework alone does not answer it. Someone must notice the issue, make the observation available and have responsibility for deciding what to do.

Consider a hypothetical training program where attendance is adequate but participants cannot use the new process because the required equipment is unavailable. Recording attendance remains useful. Recording the equipment constraint could support a different decision about procurement, sequencing or the training itself.

A sensible pilot would select a few important barriers and examine whether the record helped the team recognise and respond to them. The test is not simply whether an explanation was documented sooner. It is whether earlier access made the explanation useful to someone with an opportunity to act.

Implications for research and practice

Implementation research can inform adjustments before closure. The reviewed CFIR applications do not establish the prevalence of late evaluation across all aid programs.

Scope and limitations

This is a selective narrative brief based on the linked publications, not a systematic review or a new empirical study. Illustrative scenarios and practice implications are editorial analysis. Findings should be interpreted within the settings, methods and observation periods of their original sources.
