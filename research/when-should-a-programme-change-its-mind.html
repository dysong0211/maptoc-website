Documenting the reasons for program adaptation

An adaptation should be documented with its rationale and assessed against subsequent evidence. A reasonable decision is not automatically an effective intervention.

Evidence & Evaluation · Editorial briefSecondary synthesis · Not peer reviewed · Sources credited below

Key takeaway

An adaptation should be documented with its rationale and assessed against subsequent evidence. A reasonable decision is not automatically an effective intervention.

Evidence and context

An illustrative scenario is an outreach program designed around weekday visits. Early feedback suggests the intended participants are unavailable during those hours. The team changes its schedule. The important questions are what prompted the decision, whether the change reached different people and what the evidence showed afterwards.

The proposal for Problem-Driven Iterative Adaptation developed by Andrews, Pritchett and Woolcock emphasises locally defined problems, experimentation and feedback. It offers an approach to institutional reform; it is not an experiment demonstrating that every program benefits from constant adjustment. Andrews, Pritchett and Woolcock, 2012

My view is that a well-explained change should strengthen a program's accountability. A reader should be able to see what the original plan expected, what the team encountered, which options it considered and why the revised approach was reasonable at the time.

That also leaves room to discover that a change did not help. A record built only around defending decisions would be a poor learning tool.

Implications for research and practice

An adaptation should be documented with its rationale and assessed against subsequent evidence. A reasonable decision is not automatically an effective intervention.

Scope and limitations

This is a selective narrative brief based on the linked publications, not a systematic review or a new empirical study. Illustrative scenarios and practice implications are editorial analysis. Findings should be interpreted within the settings, methods and observation periods of their original sources.
