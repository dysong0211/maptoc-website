Simpler donor reporting and the work behind the template

Common templates can reduce inconsistency in donor requests. Their effect on total workload depends on adoption and the work required to produce the underlying evidence.

Evidence & Evaluation · Editorial briefSecondary synthesis · Not peer reviewed · Sources credited below

Key takeaway

Common templates can reduce inconsistency in donor requests. Their effect on total workload depends on adoption and the work required to produce the underlying evidence.

Evidence and context

When two donors ask for similar information in different forms, part of the reporting workload sits in translating the answer. Another part sits further back: finding the source and checking that it is still fit for the question.

GPPi's Harmonizing Donor Reporting project developed the 8+3 narrative reporting template and examined its use in a two-year pilot from 2017 to 2019. Its project account describes differing terminology and requests for overlapping information as sources of burden. It reports that most pilot participants viewed the common template as an improvement. GPPi, Harmonizing Donor Reporting

An organisation could test the workflow using a pair of real reporting requests. Which source items overlap? Which need a new interpretation? How much effort goes into retrieval, review and tailoring? Would the answer remain acceptable to each reader?

The useful result would be a clear account of total effort and answer quality. If a new archive adds more work than it removes, a more attractive reporting screen would not make the underlying case stronger.
