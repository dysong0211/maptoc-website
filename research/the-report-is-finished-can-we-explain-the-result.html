Beyond the final report: explaining program results

A complete report and an adequate explanation of results are different achievements. Evaluation requires an explicit connection between claims, evidence and the limits of interpretation.

Evidence & Evaluation · Editorial briefSecondary synthesis · Not peer reviewed · Sources credited below

Key takeaway

A complete report and an adequate explanation of results are different achievements. Evaluation requires an explicit connection between claims, evidence and the limits of interpretation.

Evidence and context

Finishing a report should leave an organisation better able to explain its work. A useful test is what the next reader can establish: what changed, which evidence supports that conclusion, and what remains uncertain.

Public research suggests those questions can remain difficult. The 2024 Aid Transparency Index found that only about a third of its 50 assessed organisations published good, current results. Separately, the UN's 2025 inspection found widespread capacity pressures from donor reporting and oversight requests. These studies examine different populations and measures; together, they suggest a problem worth investigating between reporting effort and usable evidence. Aid Transparency Index, UN JIU review

Consider an illustrative program with a complete quarterly report, an indicator spreadsheet and a folder of meeting notes. The report says delivery improved. The spreadsheet shows attendance. The meeting notes contain the explanation for a change in approach. Each document may serve its original purpose, while the connection between them still requires someone to reconstruct it.

A useful record should also retain complications: disagreement, incomplete coverage and results that did not follow the plan. Otherwise, a more polished account could simply make uncertainty harder to see.

Implications for research and practice

A complete report and an adequate explanation of results are different achievements. Evaluation requires an explicit connection between claims, evidence and the limits of interpretation.

Scope and limitations

This is a selective narrative brief based on the linked publications, not a systematic review or a new empirical study. Illustrative scenarios and practice implications are editorial analysis. Findings should be interpreted within the settings, methods and observation periods of their original sources.
