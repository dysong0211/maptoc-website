Turning lessons into decisions

Lessons become useful when their context and implications for a decision are explicit. Recording a lesson does not demonstrate that an organisation used it.

Evidence & Evaluation · Editorial briefSecondary synthesis · Not peer reviewed · Sources credited below

Key takeaway

Lessons become useful when their context and implications for a decision are explicit. Recording a lesson does not demonstrate that an organisation used it.

Evidence and context

“Engage stakeholders earlier” may be sensible advice. Without context, it gives the next program little help deciding whom to involve, when to involve them or what problem the advice is meant to prevent.

A useful lesson should carry enough information to be judged. What happened? Under which conditions? What did the team change? What evidence suggests the change helped? Where might the lesson fail to apply?

GEF's recent knowledge-management assessment identifies continuing weaknesses in capturing and sharing lessons despite formal improvements. MOPAN also found barriers to moving useful WFP knowledge across countries. These findings support attention to how knowledge reaches decisions, as well as how it is stored. GEF OPS8, knowledge management, MOPAN, WFP 2024

A future network could then help a program designer find relevant experience across programs. Relevance would need to reflect meaningful conditions, including who participated, delivery constraints and differences between locations. Similar wording alone would be a weak basis for transferring advice.

Counting lessons stored would tell us how much material accumulated. Documenting how a lesson informed the next decision would tell us whether the learning system became useful.

Implications for research and practice

Lessons become useful when their context and implications for a decision are explicit. Recording a lesson does not demonstrate that an organisation used it.

Scope and limitations

This is a selective narrative brief based on the linked publications, not a systematic review or a new empirical study. Illustrative scenarios and practice implications are editorial analysis. Findings should be interpreted within the settings, methods and observation periods of their original sources.
