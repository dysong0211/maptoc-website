Changing targets without losing the original rationale

Target changes can be justified, but their timing and rationale should be visible. Emerging findings should not silently replace original objectives.

Evidence & Evaluation · Editorial briefSecondary synthesis · Not peer reviewed · Sources credited below

Key takeaway

Target changes can be justified, but their timing and rationale should be visible. Emerging findings should not silently replace original objectives.

Evidence and context

A program may discover that an original indicator is impractical, poorly defined or insufficient for understanding the change it seeks. Revising it can be reasonable. The earlier definition and the reason for revision should remain available.

Vivalt's study of development impact evaluations examines patterns of statistical significance and how they vary across methods and publication status. The analysis concerns research reporting, not the prevalence of changing targets in aid programs. It provides a useful reason to pay attention to how the selection and presentation of results shape an evidence base. Vivalt, 2019; author manuscript

The practical implication is that a reader should be able to distinguish a criterion established before delivery from one introduced after results were observed.

An illustrative scenario is a program originally intending to measure continued service use. If it later reports only initial registrations, the record should explain the change and the question that remains unanswered. Registration may still be informative, but it does not automatically answer the earlier question.

The same principle applies to unexpected achievements. They deserve attention, but should be identified as emerging findings rather than silently replacing the original objectives.

A transparent history allows a program to learn and adapt while making its final account easier to assess. The reader can see what was promised, what changed and which conclusions the available evidence supports.

Implications for research and practice

Target changes can be justified, but their timing and rationale should be visible. Emerging findings should not silently replace original objectives.

Scope and limitations

This is a selective narrative brief based on the linked publications, not a systematic review or a new empirical study. Illustrative scenarios and practice implications are editorial analysis. Findings should be interpreted within the settings, methods and observation periods of their original sources.
