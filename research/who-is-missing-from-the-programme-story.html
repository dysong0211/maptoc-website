Who is missing from the program evidence?

Participation counts do not establish representation. Evaluation should examine whose evidence is missing and whether participation influenced decisions.

Evidence & Evaluation · Editorial briefSecondary synthesis · Not peer reviewed · Sources credited below

Key takeaway

Participation counts do not establish representation. Evaluation should examine whose evidence is missing and whether participation influenced decisions.

Evidence and context

An illustrative scenario is a program report that says stakeholders were consulted. The statement may be accurate while leaving important questions unanswered. Who attended? Who could not attend? Which concerns reached the final decision? What happened when people disagreed?

Mansuri and Rao's World Bank research synthesis examines how context, power and collective action shape participatory development. It identifies weaknesses in attention to context and in monitoring arrangements that support learning. Its argument is more complicated than either assuming participation works or dismissing it. Localizing Development: Does Participation Work?

A program record should help a reader understand whose experience informed the account. That requires care with both representation and confidentiality. A meeting note should not be made public merely because it contains useful feedback. Nor should a summary imply that everyone agreed when the source describes a narrower set of views.

A more complete program story begins with being honest about the people it can speak for. That honesty gives the team a practical next step: identify whose experience needs further attention and decide how to hear it responsibly.

Implications for research and practice

Participation counts do not establish representation. Evaluation should examine whose evidence is missing and whether participation influenced decisions.

Scope and limitations

This is a selective narrative brief based on the linked publications, not a systematic review or a new empirical study. Illustrative scenarios and practice implications are editorial analysis. Findings should be interpreted within the settings, methods and observation periods of their original sources.
